Camera 2, also 12 megapixels, now looks at the disc after the flip. It has its own lighting, a soft diffuse light, because this camera has a different job. It must clearly see the disc's reference feature, such as a hole, notch or embossed mark. Using separate cameras and lights for the two jobs means neither compromises the other. Camera 1 is lit to show the edge, and Camera 2 is lit to show the feature. ML and OpenCV methods find the disc centre and the feature, and the direction between them gives the disc's angle. Because the disc has already been flipped, the angle is measured on the face that will actually be placed, so no mirrored angle correction is needed. If the feature cannot be found clearly, the disc is rejected.
